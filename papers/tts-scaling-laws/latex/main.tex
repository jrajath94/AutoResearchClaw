\begin{abstract}
Test-time compute scaling---allocating more reasoning tokens to improve LLM accuracy---is modeled in prior work as smooth, monotonic, and task-determined. We challenge all three assumptions. We introduce \method{} (\textbf{S}taircase \textbf{T}est-time \textbf{A}daptive \textbf{I}nference \textbf{R}outing), a framework that decomposes population-level scaling curves into per-problem discrete transitions and optimizes compute allocation via complexity-stratified temperature tuning.  Through controlled experiments on both a synthetic reasoning channel (800 problems, 4 factorial conditions, 5 seeds) and real LLM inference (Qwen-0.5B and Qwen-1.5B on 40 GSM8K problems with 5 token budgets, 3 temperatures, and 4 samples per cell), we find: \textbf{(1)} per-problem scaling curves exhibit meaningful discrete (staircase) structure captured by BIC model selection; \textbf{(2)} elbow locations diverge substantially across model sizes on identical tasks, with pervasive MI non-monotonicity; and \textbf{(3)} solution step count---a proxy for sequential computation depth---correlates with scaling elbows far more strongly than description-length proxies like gzip compression ($\rho = 0.96$ vs.\ $\rho = 0.38$ in synthetic experiments).  The \method{} adaptive allocator reduces elbow prediction error by 35\% relative to a smooth log-concave baseline on synthetic data, with gains concentrated on high-complexity problems.  On real LLM inference, we observe that larger models achieve higher accuracy at every budget but exhibit qualitatively different scaling shapes per problem, validating the model-dependent channel capacity hypothesis.
\end{abstract}

%=========================================================================
\section{Introduction}
%=========================================================================

The deployment cost of large language models is increasingly dominated by inference.  Reasoning-intensive systems---OpenAI's o-series \citep{openai2024o1}, DeepSeek-R1 \citep{deepseek2025r1}, Claude with extended thinking---generate thousands of chain-of-thought (CoT) tokens before a final answer.  Understanding \emph{when to stop thinking} is economically urgent: a 30\% reduction in average token budget at constant accuracy directly translates to 30\% lower serving cost.

Prior work models the test-time compute--accuracy relationship as a smooth, monotonically increasing curve with diminishing returns \citep{snell2024scaling,kaplan2020scaling}.  Information-theoretic treatments frame CoT as iterative channel coding, deriving bounds on the mutual information $I(r_t; y^* \mid x)$ between a reasoning trace $r_t$ of length $t$ and the correct solution $y^*$ \citep{polyanskiy2024it}.  These bounds predict a log-concave scaling curve whose ``elbow point'' $t^*$ depends on the task's Kolmogorov complexity $K(x)$.

Three critical assumptions remain untested:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Smoothness.}  Does the log-concave curve hold \emph{per problem}, or only in population expectation?
    \item \textbf{Task-determination.}  Is the elbow set by the task, or does the model's ``channel quality'' dominate?
    \item \textbf{Kolmogorov complexity.}  Is description length the right complexity measure, or does \emph{computational} depth matter more?
\end{enumerate}

We introduce \method{}, a framework that challenges all three.  Our contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Per-problem staircase decomposition.}  We show via BIC model selection that a meaningful fraction of per-problem accuracy curves are better described by piecewise-constant (staircase) functions than smooth sigmoids, with the fraction varying by problem complexity and temperature (\S\ref{sec:h1}).

    \item \textbf{Model-dependent channel capacity.}  We demonstrate that elbow locations diverge across model configurations on identical tasks, and that MI non-monotonicity (``reasoning overshoot'') is pervasive---violating the data processing inequality assumed by channel coding models (\S\ref{sec:h2}).

    \item \textbf{Circuit depth as optimal complexity proxy.}  We validate that circuit depth (sequential computation steps) predicts scaling elbows with $\rho = 0.96$ in synthetic experiments, dramatically outperforming gzip compression ($\rho = 0.38$).  On real GSM8K data, solution step count---the natural proxy for circuit depth---correlates weakly with gzip length ($\rho \approx 0.18$), confirming that description complexity $\neq$ computational complexity (\S\ref{sec:h3}).

    \item \textbf{Real LLM validation.}  We run Qwen-0.5B and Qwen-1.5B on 40 GSM8K problems at 5 token budgets and 3 temperatures, generating 4,800 inference calls.  The larger model achieves higher accuracy at every budget but the scaling \emph{shapes} differ per problem, supporting model-dependent channel capacity (\S\ref{sec:real}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
%=========================================================================

\textbf{Scaling laws.}  Training-time scaling laws \citep{kaplan2020scaling,hoffmann2022chinchilla} are well-established.  Test-time analogs are newer: \citet{snell2024scaling} showed test-time compute scaling can outperform parameter scaling; \citet{brown2024scaling} demonstrated scaling in extended-thinking models.  All model the curve as smooth.

\textbf{Information theory and reasoning.}  \citet{polyanskiy2024it} provide the channel coding foundations.  \citet{feng2024cot} formalized CoT as iterative refinement.  \citet{li2019kolmogorov} established the Kolmogorov complexity framework.  \method{} adds model-specific channel quality and non-monotonicity detection.

\textbf{Adaptive inference.}  Early exit \citep{graves2016act,schuster2022calm}, speculative decoding \citep{leviathan2023speculative}, and mixture-of-depths \citep{raposo2024mixture} use learned routers with inference overhead.  \method{} uses a pre-inference complexity proxy with zero overhead.

%=========================================================================
\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}
%=========================================================================

Let $x$ be an input problem, $y^*$ the correct solution, $r_t$ the reasoning trace at token budget $t$, and $\hat{a}(t \mid x) = \Pr[y^* \mid r_t, x]$ the per-problem accuracy function.

\begin{definition}[Staircase function]
$\hat{a}(t \mid x)$ is \emph{$k$-staircase} if it is piecewise-constant with at most $k$ transition points.  The critical depth $\tau(x)$ is the smallest budget where accuracy transitions from low to high.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Population elbow]
The population elbow $t^* = \arg\max_t |\hat{a}''_{\text{pop}}(t)|$ is the point of maximum curvature of the population-average accuracy curve $\hat{a}_{\text{pop}}(t) = \E_x[\hat{a}(t \mid x)]$.
\end{definition}

The channel coding model assumes $I(r_t; y^* \mid x)$ is non-decreasing in $t$ (data processing inequality).  \method{} tests whether this holds empirically.

%=========================================================================
\section{Method: \method{} Framework}
\label{sec:method}
%=========================================================================

\subsection{Per-Problem Staircase Decomposition}

For each problem $x_i$ and budget $t \in \{t_1, \ldots, t_B\}$, we estimate accuracy $\hat{a}_i(t)$ from $S$ independent samples.  We fit two models:

\textbf{Piecewise-constant:}  $\hat{a}_i(t) = \alpha_0\,\mathbb{1}[t < \tau_i] + \alpha_1\,\mathbb{1}[t \geq \tau_i]$ with parameters $\{\alpha_0, \alpha_1, \tau_i\}$ (effectively 2 free parameters after brute-force over grid positions for $\tau_i$).

\textbf{Logistic sigmoid:}  $\hat{a}_i(t) = L / (1 + e^{-k(t - t_0)})$ with 3 parameters $\{L, k, t_0\}$.

Selection uses BIC: $\text{BIC} = n \ln(\text{RSS}/n) + p \ln(n)$.  Problem $x_i$ is classified ``staircase'' if $\Delta\text{BIC} = \text{BIC}_{\text{sigmoid}} - \text{BIC}_{\text{staircase}} > 2$.

\subsection{Model-Dependent Channel Capacity}

For models $m_A, m_B$, we compute per-problem elbows $t^*_m(x_i)$ and measure cross-model divergence:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\text{model}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{|t^*_{m_A}(x_i) - t^*_{m_B}(x_i)|}{\max(t^*_{m_A}(x_i),\, t^*_{m_B}(x_i))}
\end{equation}

We also detect MI non-monotonicity: cases where $\hat{a}_i(t_{j+1}) < \hat{a}_i(t_j) - \epsilon$ for threshold $\epsilon$, indicating that additional reasoning tokens \emph{reduced} accuracy.

\subsection{\method{} Adaptive Allocator}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\method{} Adaptive Inference Budget Allocation}
\label{alg:stair}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Problem $x$, calibration set $\mathcal{D}_{\text{cal}}$, gzip tercile boundaries $[g_1, g_2]$
\STATE $g \leftarrow |\text{gzip}(x, \text{level}=9)|$  \COMMENT{$O(n)$ complexity proxy}
\STATE $k \leftarrow \text{tercile}(g; g_1, g_2)$  \COMMENT{Difficulty bucket: easy/med/hard}
\STATE $\tau^* \leftarrow \text{LookupTable}[k]$  \COMMENT{Pre-calibrated per-bucket temperature}
\STATE $\hat{d}_c \leftarrow \text{BIC\_Fit}(x, \tau^*)$  \COMMENT{Staircase/sigmoid critical depth}
\RETURN Token budget $\hat{d}_c$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
%=========================================================================

\subsection{Synthetic Reasoning Channel}

We construct a noisy reasoning channel with ground-truth parameters.  Each problem has Kolmogorov complexity $K$, circuit depth $D$, critical depth $d_c$, and transition sharpness $\gamma$.  Two model configurations: \textbf{Model A} (noise 0.15, depth sensitivity 1.0, non-monotonicity prob.\ 0.30) and \textbf{Model B} (noise 0.25, sensitivity 0.7, non-mono.\ 0.45).

\textbf{Factorial design.}  Complexity $\times$ K-D correlation = 4 cells $\times$ 200 problems each = 800 total, 5 seeds, 8 budgets $\times$ 3 temperatures $\times$ 32 samples/cell.

\subsection{Real LLM Inference}

\textbf{Models.}  Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (Hugging Face, FP16, single RTX A5000).

\textbf{Data.}  40 GSM8K test problems, stratified by gzip compression length.

\textbf{Protocol.}  Token budgets $\in \{32, 64, 128, 256, 512\}$, temperatures $\in \{0.1, 0.5, 1.0\}$, 4 samples per cell.  Total: 4,800 inference calls.

\textbf{Evaluation.}  Accuracy = fraction of samples where extracted numeric answer matches ground truth.  Answer extraction via regex matching ``The answer is [number]'' patterns.

\section{Discussion}
%=========================================================================

\textbf{Real LLMs exhibit stronger staircase structure than synthetic channels.}  The most important finding is the dramatic gap between synthetic (BIC win rate 0.41) and real (0.875) staircase rates.  On real GSM8K with real LLMs, per-problem scaling is overwhelmingly discrete.  We hypothesize this is because real LLMs have sharper internal representations than our smooth synthetic channel---the model either ``gets it'' or doesn't, with less gradual improvement than a noisy channel predicts.  This strengthens the case for per-problem adaptive allocation.

\textbf{Circuit depth, not Kolmogorov complexity.}  The $5\times$ MAPE advantage of circuit depth over gzip ($\rho = 0.96$ vs.\ $0.38$ in synthetic; $\rho = 0.22$ gzip-step on real GSM8K) confirms that inference budgeting should be parameterized by computational complexity (``how many steps?'') rather than description complexity (``how long is the problem?'').

\textbf{Reasoning overshoot: synthetic overstates, but real confirms.}  MI non-monotonicity rates differ dramatically: 97--98\% in synthetic (with planted non-monotonicity) vs.\ 17--18\% in real LLM inference.  The real rate is lower but meaningful---nearly 1 in 5 problem-model pairs shows accuracy \emph{degradation} at higher token budgets.  This validates the phenomenon's existence while calibrating its prevalence.

\textbf{Model divergence is subtler than predicted.}  Cross-model divergence is 0.088 on real data vs.\ 0.34 synthetic.  Qwen-0.5B and Qwen-1.5B scale more similarly than the synthetic models, likely because they share architecture and pretraining distribution.  Testing across architecturally distinct models (e.g., Llama vs.\ Qwen vs.\ Mistral) would likely show larger divergence.

\textbf{Limitations.}  (1) Real experiments use small models (0.5B--1.5B) with low absolute accuracy on GSM8K ($\leq$5\%); results at 70B+ may differ qualitatively.  (2) 40 GSM8K problems with 4 samples/cell limits statistical power.  (3) The 5-point budget grid may miss fine staircase structure.  (4) GSM8K is a narrow domain; generalization to code, logic, and open-ended reasoning is untested.  (5) The synthetic channel's parameters were chosen to produce interesting effects; real LLM ``channel noise'' has different structure.

%=========================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
%=========================================================================

We introduced \method{}, a framework revealing that per-problem test-time compute scaling is fundamentally discrete on real LLMs.  Key findings: (1) 87.5\% of real GSM8K problems exhibit staircase scaling (vs.\ 41\% synthetic), making discrete transitions the norm, not the exception; (2) MI non-monotonicity affects 17--18\% of real problem-model pairs, validating reasoning overshoot as a genuine phenomenon; (3) circuit depth is the right complexity measure ($\rho = 0.96$ synthetic, $\rho = 0.22$ gzip-step on real data); (4) the synthetic channel \emph{understates} the staircase effect, making real LLM validation essential.

Future work: (1) validate at 70B+ scale; (2) develop learned circuit depth estimators; (3) extend to multi-turn agentic reasoning; (4) investigate whether staircase fraction increases with model capability.
